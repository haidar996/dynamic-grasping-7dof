Replaces `FINAL_PLAN_2026-10-28.md`. Same goal and phases; Phase 0 is redone because of the audit
(see `RESUME.md`). Rule: one step at a time, explained, user decides before code runs.
All numbers in this plan are **targets or unknowns**, never results, until a step measures them.

**Goal:** estimate ball position/velocity from a simulated overhead depth camera, in a 2.5×2.5 m arena with
a static Franka arm at the centre; compare hand-made filters, gray-box learning and learned filters; then use
the best estimate in the chapter-1 grasp planners.

**Fixed setup (decided):**
- Arena 2.5×2.5 m centred on the origin, walls at ±1.25 m (ball centre limit ±1.22 m), planar ball, radius 0.03 m.
- Ball physics in `arena_sim.py`: friction μ ∈ [0.2, 1.5] m/s², restitution E ∈ [0.5, 0.95], same E for walls and arm.
- Arm: frozen at Q_HOME at (0, 0), no joints; the ball bounces off the base box (x −0.154..0.072, y ±0.095 m).
- Launches: 50 speeds (0.1–5.0 m/s) × 10 = 500, random start (outside the no-start zone around the arm) and direction.
- Camera: one overhead depth camera at 3.0 m, **480×480, FOV 49°**, 60 Hz (locked 2026-10-30).
- CPU only, ≤ 3–4 parallel workers on this laptop.

### 0.2 FOV method (proposed, needs OK)
The FOV only trades **coverage** against **pixels per metre** (camera position, so arm occlusion, does not change).
The ball does not blur in MuJoCo renders, so motion adds nothing → a **static grid test** measures exactly what matters, fast:
1. Place the ball at a grid of points over the whole reachable area (5 cm spacing ≈ 2,400 points, incl. walls, corners,
   and next to the arm). Same points for every FOV (paired).
2. FOVs: **40–70° in 1° steps** (31 values, user request). Render each point once per FOV (~5 min total, 3 workers).
3. Per FOV measure: % of points detected (outside the arm shadow), worst margin to the image edge (px),
   ball size (px), position error with the ideal camera and with the `noise` sensor condition (median, p95, corners),
   and **arena fill** = % of image pixels showing the arena floor (user request: see the arena, not much around it).
4. **Selection rule, agreed before looking at the results:** hard requirement = every non-occluded point detected with
   ≥ 10 px margin to the image edge; among FOVs that pass, pick the one with the **highest arena fill**
   (= the narrowest passing FOV). Note: 640×480 is 4:3, the arena is square → fill ≤ ~75%; a square 480×480 image
   would remove the side strips (separate decision after the results).
5. Output: table + plot + `camera_settings_locked.yaml`.
